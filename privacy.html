Privacy Policy

Effective 19 November 2026; YouTube section added 20 November 2026.

Who this covers

JARVIS Content Manager ("JARVIS") is a personal desktop assistant used only by its owner on their own Windows computer. It has no other users.

What JARVIS receives from TikTok

  • Access and refresh tokens, issued by TikTok when the owner signs in and approves access.
  • The account's open ID, display name and username (scopes user.info.basic and user.info.profile), used only to label the connection and link to the owner's own posts.
  • Upload and processing status for videos the owner chooses to send (scope video.upload).

What JARVIS receives from YouTube (Google)

  • Access and refresh tokens, issued by Google when the owner signs in to their own YouTube channel and approves access.
  • The channel's ID and title (scope youtube.readonly), used to confirm which of the owner's channels is connected and to show its own videos and statistics.
  • The channel's own YouTube Analytics reports, such as views, watch time and retention (scopes yt-analytics.readonly and yt-analytics-monetary.readonly), shown only to the owner in JARVIS.
  • Upload status for videos the owner approves for their own channel (scope youtube.upload). JARVIS never uploads without the owner's approval of that specific video.

JARVIS's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Where it is kept

Tokens are stored only on the owner's computer, in Windows Credential Manager. They are never written to logs, shown on screen, or sent anywhere except back to the platform that issued them (TikTok or Google). There is no JARVIS server and no cloud copy.

Sharing

JARVIS does not sell, rent or share any data, and contains no advertising or analytics trackers. Videos are sent only to the owner's own TikTok or YouTube account, and only when the owner chooses to.

Deleting data

Removing the TikTok sign-in (tiktok-oauth) in JARVIS under Settings → Credentials deletes the stored tokens. Access can also be removed in the TikTok app (Settings and privacy → Security and permissions → Apps). Removing the YouTube sign-in (youtube-oauth) deletes the stored Google tokens; access can also be removed at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
